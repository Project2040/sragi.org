# SRAGI® Contributor Rights Framework

## Purpose

SRAGI accepts contributions only where the rights chain remains clear enough to support the licensing model of the resulting artifact.

This document is a contributor-rights framework and contributor notice. It is not, by itself, a signed Contributor License Agreement for a specific contribution.

## Core rule

A contribution may be included in an artifact offered under `LicenseRef-SRAGI-Commercial` only where Neptunia Media AS has sufficient rights to license that contribution under the applicable commercial terms.

Contributions for which sufficient commercial licensing rights have not been obtained must not be represented as available under `LicenseRef-SRAGI-Commercial`.

## Provenance

Contributions should preserve, as applicable:

- contributor identity or recorded provenance;
- source and origin;
- applicable license or rights statement;
- third-party notices;
- sufficient evidence of authority to contribute.

## Rights discipline

Contributor terms should obtain only the rights necessary for the applicable SRAGI licensing and distribution model while preserving clear provenance and authorship records.

Nothing in this framework transfers ownership merely by submitting an idea, issue, discussion comment or other material unless an applicable contribution process or agreement expressly provides otherwise.

## Commercial relicensing

Where a contribution is intended for an artifact that uses a dual-license expression such as:

`CC-BY-SA-4.0 OR LicenseRef-SRAGI-Commercial`

Neptunia Media AS must have sufficient rights for both paths before representing the contribution as available under both paths.

## Third-party material

Contributors must not knowingly submit third-party material on terms incompatible with the target artifact. Third-party rights remain governed by their applicable licenses, agreements and law.

## Future contributor agreements

Specific repositories, projects or contribution classes may require a separate signed or click-through Contributor License Agreement, Developer Certificate of Origin, assignment, or other rights instrument. The applicable contribution process will identify that requirement where used.
